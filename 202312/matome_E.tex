Obsolescence of highly cited papers:
We observed that while highly cited papers maintain top rankings for extended periods due to the cumulative effect of citations, they eventually undergo decay and yield their positions to new cohorts of papers. The duration until this transition was exceptionally long, reaching over 50 years in some cases. Among the set of papers where obsolescence was confirmed, the peak year tended to be longer compared to existing reports.

Journal characteristics of highly cited papers:
It became clear that highly cited papers are not necessarily published in top-tier, high-Impact Factor (IF) journals. In other words, although journals with higher IFs are generally more heavily represented in citation databases, highly cited papers are not exclusively confined to such journals. Regarding journal preferences for highly cited papers, no single dominant journal was identified; however, analysis using KLD revealed that citations are more concentrated toward certain journals compared to other groups. This tendency was found to strengthen over time.

Thematic characteristics of highly cited papers and cited themes:
We found that highly cited papers tend to focus on concentrated research themes, are likely to be cited across diverse thematic areas, and the citing papers themselves also exhibit concentrated thematic profiles. While the diversity of both cited and citing themes appears low when examined individually, the degree of transition from cited themes to citing themes is relatively high. If this transition characteristic influences obsolescence, papers on topics that attract interest across various research domains may continue to be cited for a longer period. If these characteristics manifest at the journal level, foundational or comprehensive journals may see their articles cited for relatively longer periods, exerting a substantial long-term impact that cannot be captured by short-term metrics such as the Impact Factor.

Long-term trends:
Although various indices were employed in this study, few exhibited clear long-term trends. However, a gradual increase over time was observed in the entropy of citing themes, cited themes, and citing-cited theme pairs, as well as in the journal preference patterns of highly cited papers.

Impact of cumulative citation data:
Analysis of rank correlation with varying cumulative citation windows revealed that using cumulative citations increases the variability of rankings. In other words, utilizing cumulative citation data enhances the dynamics when evaluated on an ordinal scale.

What are the most-cited papers?
Finally, we provide an overview of the three papers that emerged as the most-cited in this study.
\begin{itemize}
\item "Qualitative analysis of proteins: a partition chromatographic method using paper" is a methodological paper, and the "paper chromatography" technique introduced herein is famously known for having been developed by its authors.
\item "Protein measurement with the Folin phenol regent" is a methodological paper, widely known as the "Lowry method" named after its authors.
\item "Cleavage of structural protein during the assembly of the head of bacteriophage T4" is a paper introducing a laboratory method, well known for laying the groundwork for the current "SDS-PAGE" technique.
\end{itemize}
Note that the second and third papers are also featured in the Nature article "The top 100 papers" \cite{Nor1}.
